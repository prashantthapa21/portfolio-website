Week 1 • Data Analysis

Why data cleaning matters more than it looks

When people first start learning data analysis, they often get excited about charts, dashboards, and final insights. But the real work begins much earlier: cleaning the data.

Data is rarely ready as-is

Raw datasets usually come with missing values, duplicates, inconsistent labels, unexpected formatting, and sometimes obvious errors. If we skip this stage, the final analysis can be misleading even when the visualizations look polished.

Cleaning improves accuracy

Good analysis depends on trustworthy numbers. A single inconsistent category or a blank field can quietly distort trends, rates, and summaries. Cleaning helps fix these gaps before we draw conclusions.

It helps reveal the real story

Clean data gives us a clearer view of the actual pattern in the dataset. Without this step, we might misread noise as insight or miss the signal completely. That is why cleaning is often the difference between a confusing chart and a meaningful one.

What I learned

  • Start with structure: check for missing, duplicate, or inconsistent values.
  • Standardize names, dates, and categories before analysis.
  • Always validate assumptions before making decisions from the data.
  • Good analysis is not only about tools; it is about careful thinking.
