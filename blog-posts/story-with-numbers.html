Week 2 • Excel

Learning to tell a story with numbers

Numbers alone do not create understanding. A chart becomes useful when it tells a clear story, connects to context, and helps people make decisions based on evidence.

Charts are not just decoration

When I first started working with spreadsheets, I often focused on whether a chart looked attractive. But over time I learned that the real value is in clarity. A chart should highlight a question, explain a pattern, and reduce confusion.

Context matters

Raw numbers can mislead if we do not understand the situation behind them. The best analysis connects figures to a real problem: what changed, why it changed, and what action should follow.

Good storytelling makes analysis useful

The best dashboards and reports are not the ones with the most data points—they are the ones that make the important message obvious. A clear heading, a focused chart, and a meaningful insight often matter more than adding more visuals.

What I am practicing

  • Choosing simple visuals that fit the data.
  • Highlighting trends instead of overwhelming the audience.
  • Using labels and context to make insights easier to understand.
  • Turning numbers into a meaningful narrative.
